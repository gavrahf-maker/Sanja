How to speak clearly, distinctly and beautifully?

If you speak clearly and distinctly, you leave a very good impression on the person you're talking to, and the idea you want to get across will be clear. That's why I advise you to set aside a little time to improve the way you speak.

  • The first thing to do is take a newspaper article, a book or anything else you can use to practise your reading.
  • Before you start reading, open your mouth as wide as you can and say each vowel – a, e, i, o, u – for a long time, stretching your mouth all the way. The air should go only forwards, as if there were a little ball in front of you that your breath has to turn.
  • Before you start reading, calm your thoughts. Calm down and take a deep breath. Make sure you have enough air in your lungs for the sentence that follows.
  • Start reading. How did the first sentence you read sound? Could it sound better? I believe it could.
  • Now read the same sentence again, but twice as slowly. You will probably feel the urge to speed up, because reading that slowly can be boring. :) But don't. Reading slowly has a purpose, so resist the feeling that you should speed up. If you rush, some word will surely remain unclear – and that shouldn't happen. Every word must be pronounced slowly enough to be clear.
  • Now read that sentence again and PRONOUNCE EVERY SYLLABLE (part) of each word CLEARLY. Say each syllable separately and slowly, and practise until every one is clear and distinct.
  • Gradually speed up and shorten the pauses between words until you manage to speak clearly at a normal pace.

When breathing and speaking, be sure to gently stop the airflow on tricky consonants such as s, z, r, t, ch, sh… When said quickly, these are the consonants that make a word unclear. That's why the difference between all the consonants, as well as between all the vowels, should be clear, and every single letter – every sound, vowel or consonant – deserves enough attention.

If you notice that you "flew over" any syllable, go back, slow down and say that syllable more slowly.

Practise whenever you're alone – in the car or walking down the street, while cleaning or ironing, or standing in front of the mirror. Always set aside a little time; it can be just a few seconds or minutes. What matters is that you think about it every day.

You can also slow down and pay attention to syllables when talking to other people. You'll see that you make remarkable progress if you seriously invest some time in your speaking skills.

It can be incredibly useful to give your words another second or two so they are pronounced completely correctly.

Deep breathing can help you slow down your speech, so give it special attention. Taking a deep breath before you speak will let you talk more slowly and have enough air for the whole phrase. You also need enough air because, when speaking, air leaves the mouth continuously and EVENLY.

If you speak more slowly, your voice will stay calm and you will sound clearer.

Pauses between sentences give you more time to think while speaking, so you can be more sure of what you're going to say.

Speak as if you were writing an essay or a letter: patiently, cheerfully and precisely.

Try to break your thoughts into logical, well-rounded parts. Always stay focused on the essence of what you want to say.

Expand your vocabulary. One right word can be much clearer than a flood of less precise ones.

Try to find the exact word you're looking for – and use it effectively.

Be careful not to use words incorrectly or in the wrong context; this could undermine the clarity of what you're trying to say, and you may not be understood the way you intended.

A word of caution: make sure the people you're talking to know the words you use with them.

Keep your audience in mind. Use simpler words if necessary.

Reading is a great way to expand your vocabulary. Read books, articles, essays; read things that interest or fascinate you.

Whenever you come across a word you don't know yet, look up what it means.

Try keeping a list of beautiful, useful, effective words. The more you use them in the right context, the more naturally they'll come to you – and your choice of words will get even better.

Think before you speak. Preparing what you're going to say reduces the risk of getting flustered and saying something you didn't plan. Even if you don't plan the exact words, take a moment to review your ideas, ask yourself what the essence of what you want to say is, and keep that idea in your head. Because the essence matters most of all.

Say the words quietly and slowly to yourself before you say them out loud. This can help you pronounce them correctly.

Speak with the right intonation. If you want to ask a question, your pitch should rise at the end of the sentence. Statements should end with confidence.

Pay attention to which syllables are stressed in a sentence.

And finally, emphasise all the words that carry the essence of the sentence.

Yours,Sanja Trifunović Gerginov
