How to overcome the fear of performing in public?

Performing in public can be a stressful experience if we have negative expectations in our head and fear what the people in the audience will think. There are ways to help you finally relax and gain more confidence on stage.

1. Prepare

Give yourself enough time to prepare absolutely every detail of the upcoming performance. And make sure that some time before – a day or two before the performance – you are already ready and still have enough time for anything unforeseen. Give yourself enough time to learn everything you will present. Give what you need to present enough time to settle, so that all that information is familiar, well established and close to you. What you present must never be a bogeyman for you. If it's a performance, what you perform should be fully absorbed, thoroughly familiar – a part of you. If you sing, work on those songs over a longer period, practise enough, explore what else could be improved, and seek professional support.

Give yourself time for creativity, to immerse yourself in what you are presenting and to become one with it. Because it's your story you're telling, and it's the reason you're there.

Try to anticipate as many situations as possible that could come up unexpectedly, and prepare for them too. The more organised you are, the less nervous you'll be.

2. Calm your breathing

Whenever you feel fear right before the performance, breathe in deeply and slowly while counting to 4, then hold your breath while counting to 3, and slowly and evenly breathe out while counting to 5. When we are afraid, our breath stops and we tense up – and if we hold our breath, we hold on to the tension too.

Repeat this exercise until you feel calm. Here too, time plays the main role: if you start preparing earlier, you have time to use your breathing to positively influence the anxiety that comes up when you think about the upcoming performance.

3. Stop negative expectations

The moment you experience a negative thought, you gain the power to steer it. You have two options: accept it, believe it and let it steer you – or accept it so that you can steer it. It can come, but it mustn't upset your plans. Simply think about what you can control and what depends on you, and work on that at every possible moment. If you have worked with dedication and given it your all, success will come. Things you can't control are in the minority anyway; there's no point in thinking about them.

4. Analyse your goals

Think about and write down the exact goals of this performance – what you would like to achieve and what impression you want to leave. How exactly would you like your performance to look? What is the most basic thing you want to achieve, without which this performance wouldn't be a success? Write it all down, and then take care of every single detail needed to reach your goals. Don't forget that all these details require the preparation time we talked about at the beginning.

5. What doesn't kill us makes us stronger

Keep in mind the whole time that the situation ahead cannot kill us, and it will pass. Don't forget that, however important the event, afterwards everything will be relatively the same: this situation is not an ending, not a finish line – life goes on. After the performance we'll have the chance to correct whatever needs correcting, to progress and to become even better at what we do.

6. Be kind to yourself

Only experience will make your performance flawless. Don't judge yourself harshly, but work on making your current presentation as good as possible. If you work enough for the performance, you will surely be satisfied. When you have truly given your best, leave the rest of the space to experience and time to fill in any remaining gaps, if there are any.

The more experience we have, the smaller the stage fright. But if you give yourself enough time, these tips will help you succeed at every performance. Make an effort to build your self-confidence solely through persistent work on yourself – because there simply is no other trick – and don't let unjustified fears spoil what you are fighting for.

Yours,Sanja Trifunović Gerginov
